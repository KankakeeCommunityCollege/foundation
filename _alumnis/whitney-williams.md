**Whitney Williams: Finding Her Way Back After the Storm**

After surviving the tornado that obliterated Joplin, Missouri in 2011, Whitney Williams returned to Illinois, where she had grown up. She worked in retail for a short time before completing Certified Nursing Assistant training at KCC in 2016.

“I loved what I did, but I just felt like something was missing,” Williams said.

That feeling eventually led Williams back to KCC.

“I already knew KCC was a good school,” she said. “It was sort of like a home away from home. Even if you have an issue with everyday life, they are there to help.”

Williams applied to the Respiratory Therapist program but didn’t pass the entrance exam on her first attempt. Devastated but determined to change her life, she went to see the program director, Jackie Montemeyer. After learning more about Williams and seeing her determination, Montemayer talked with her colleagues and returned with good news.

“She told me, ‘We’re going to give you a chance,’” Williams said. “She said, ‘It speaks volumes that you came down here and you came to find me to plead your case because you really want to get in this program.’”

That moment became a defining memory of Williams’ time at KCC. From then on, their check-ins were always heartfelt.

“As a professor, sometimes our job isn’t to measure potential, but to recognize it,” Montemayer said. “Whitney taught me that success is not determined by where you start, but by how relentless, resilient, and determined you pursue where it is you want to go.”

“Every time we did a check-in, we cried because she remembered the first day she met me,” Williams said. The program tested Williams physically, financially and emotionally.

“There were times I felt like I wanted to give up just from exhaustion,” Williams said. “I didn’t have full support from my family, so getting that kind of encouragement from people I had never met before meant everything.”

Williams also found the structure and support she needed to stay on track. Because she had completed prerequisites earlier, many remaining courses focused on respiratory care. The cohort was small, and her instructors were accessible for help.

“If I felt like I was struggling, both instructors would sit down and help,” she said. “There was no hesitation if you needed to call them or text them. If you’re not at school, they would answer.”

During her final semester, Williams was out of money. A scholarship from the Women’s Giving Circle helped her finish.

“The scholarship came at the right time,” Williams said. “I didn’t know how I was going to pay for that last semester. I wouldn’t be where I am right now without that support, and I just want to say thank you from the bottom of my heart.”

Williams graduated from KCC in December 2024 and passed her boards in January 2025. She is currently a respiratory therapist at Riverside Medical Center, where she previously worked as a CNA.

To students who are struggling, Williams says: “Ask questions. If you’re struggling, speak up. If you have something going on personally, go talk to somebody because you never know the help they can give you. They have resources for everything.”

The impact of KCC has extended beyond Williams’ own experience. Her daughter is currently completing prerequisites to enroll in the KCC Radiology program.
