**Yolanda Rios: Finding Her Place in the Legal Field**

Yolanda Rios was raising two young children and trying to build a more stable future when she returned to Kankakee Community College.

Already with an Associate in General Studies, Rios found a clear direction in KCC’s Paralegal/Legal Assistant Studies program. It connected her interest in legal work with a practical path forward.

“My teachers were either judges or attorneys,” she said. “What they do in their day job is what they were teaching us at night. They gave us real scenarios and real experience.”

Rios attended networking events with attorneys and judges, including lunch with Illinois Supreme Court justices. She also participated in a professional etiquette dinner where students practiced formal dining and professional conversation.

“It gave me self-confidence,” she said. “I can actually sit with these people at the same table and learn from them and be successful like them. You no longer feel like you’re out of place.”

Rios gained hands-on experience during an internship with Prairie State Legal Services, assisting with legal petitions and accompanying an attorney to court. She speaks Spanish, and helped interpret for clients who needed support understanding the legal process.

“You’re going to court, seeing how it works,” she said. “You’re in the middle of it. The experience is very hands-on.”

During this time, Rios had a full course load, worked full time and had jobs at Bed Bath & Beyond and Uber. By her final semester, the demands of school, work, parenting and bills had reached a breaking point.

“I was trying to put myself through school, but I just wasn’t making enough,” Rios said. “I was almost at that finish line. It came down to either I quit school or I pay these bills.”

Then, Rios received a scholarship from the KCC Foundation’s Women’s Giving Circle.

“My scholarship gave me the boost I needed,” she said. “It allowed me to concentrate on school so I could graduate and get my job. And that’s what I did.”

In 2017, she completed an Associate in Applied Science in Paralegal/Legal Assistant Studies. After graduation, she worked as a paralegal at the Kankakee County State’s Attorney’s Office and then later the Kankakee County Health Department.

Today, Rios is a paralegal in Fort Riley, Kansas. Her husband is stationed there with the U.S. Army.

Rios also credits Dr. Kristine Condon, who led the paralegal program, with setting a high standard for students.

“It was never okay to be mediocre,” Rios said. “It was ‘Push yourself harder. Push yourself to be better.’ You felt honored. You felt pride to be there.”

That preparation continues to matter in her work today.

Looking back, Rios is proud of the skills she gained, the confidence she built and the sense of belonging she developed in the legal field. She also remembers what it meant for her children to watch her graduate.

“My kids went to my graduation, and it was amazing,” she said. “I was so proud.”

For Rios, completing her degree represented years of work, sacrifice and determination. It also reflected the impact of people who chose to support students they may never meet.

“The scholarships are necessary and the scholarships are a blessing,” she said. “If donors ever wonder if they made a difference, they did with me. Their help is why I was able to graduate. I could not have done it without whoever donated that money to help me.”
